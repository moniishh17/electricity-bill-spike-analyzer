How to use BillSpike

Six steps from your paper bill to an answer. Bill layouts can change, so if a label looks different on your bill, look for the same figure under a similar name.

  1. Gather at least four bills

    Use your last 4 to 6 bi-monthly bills: paper copies, the SMS or email, or your online account. More bills give a better idea of what is normal. For the year-on-year check, include bills from a year ago too.

  2. Find the billing period

    Each bill shows the dates of its two-month period, or the previous and present reading dates. In BillSpike pick the From month and the To month. The days fill in by themselves; change them only if your bill shows a different number.

  3. Enter the units used

    Units are the present meter reading minus the previous reading, and are usually printed on the bill as the units consumed. Type that number in the Units box.

  4. Enter the amount you paid

    Use the total payable for that bill. If the bill also lists arrears from earlier bills or a late fee, subtract them first, because BillSpike compares only the current charges with the tariff.

  5. Set your connected load

    The connected (sanctioned) load in kW is printed on the bill and sets the small fixed charge. If you cannot find it, leave 1 kW; the fixed charge is small, so the estimate will still be close.

  6. Press Analyze bills and read the results

    Check the red spikes, the gap between Billed and Tariff says, and the forecast. A large gap can mean a wrong reading, arrears or a tariff change. Mid-cycle, use Cliff Guard to stay under 500 units.

New to electricity bills? Start here

What is a unit?

One unit is one kilowatt-hour: a 1,000 W appliance running for an hour. Your bill is mostly units times a rate per unit.

Why do bills come every two months?

TNPDCL reads domestic meters about every two months, so each bill covers roughly 60 days.

What are slabs?

Units are charged in bands. Only the units inside a band pay that band's rate, not the whole bill.

What is the 500-unit cliff?

As publicly reported for the scheme from 10 May 2026: up to 500 units, the first 200 are free. Above 500, only 100 are free and extra units fall in ₹8.40+ slabs, so the bill jumps.

What are the fixed charge and connected load?

Each bill has a small fixed charge based on your connected load (kW, printed on the bill), plus a fuel surcharge of about ₹0.20 per unit that TNERC revises quarterly.

How do I read my bill?

Present reading minus previous reading is the units used. Check the dates are about 60 days apart and look for arrears from earlier bills.

How can I cut units?

Set the AC to 24–26 °C, switch off standby devices, use LEDs, run the geyser on a timer and use the estimator below to find the big users.

    Domestic (LT-IA), per bi-monthly bill. Rates from public reports; effective 10 May 2026.

    Fixed charge: ₹30 up to 500 W, ₹45 up to 1 kW, +₹30 per extra kW per bill. Fuel surcharge about ₹0.20 per unit.
