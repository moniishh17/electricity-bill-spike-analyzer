New to electricity bills? Start here

What is a unit?

One unit is one kilowatt-hour: a 1,000 W appliance running for an hour. Your bill is mostly units times a rate per unit.

Why do bills come every two months?

TNPDCL reads domestic meters about every two months, so each bill covers roughly 60 days.

What are slabs?

Units are charged in bands. Only the units inside a band pay that band's rate, not the whole bill.

What is the 500-unit cliff?

As publicly reported for the scheme from 10 May 2026: up to 500 units, the first 200 are free. Above 500, only 100 are free and extra units fall in ₹8.40+ slabs, so the bill jumps.

What are the fixed charge and connected load?

Each bill has a small fixed charge based on your connected load (kW, printed on the bill), plus a fuel surcharge of about ₹0.20 per unit that TNERC revises quarterly.

How do I read my bill?

Present reading minus previous reading is the units used. Check the dates are about 60 days apart and look for arrears from earlier bills.

How can I cut units?

Set the AC to 24–26 °C, switch off standby devices, use LEDs, run the geyser on a timer and use the estimator below to find the big users.
